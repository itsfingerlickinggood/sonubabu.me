Evaluation engine · 2026

A red-team engine for agents

A harness for attacking tool-using AI agents. It is scored on what the agent does, not on what it says.

  • 5 of 6capture-the-flag levels broken
  • 9 of 10epochs passed, twice, on the email-agent test
  • ~1,900attempts on level 6, no pass
  • ~6,600lines of code, with tests

One rule shapes the design

Writing attacks and sending them are separate jobs. Attacker models only write text. They never touch the browser, a session or a credential.

  1. Generate

    text only

    Several attacker models in different roles. A refusal detector watches each one.

  2. Gate

    checkpoint

    A rules linter blocks anything out of bounds before it can be sent.

  3. Rank

    text only

    Spend the budget on payload families not yet tried. The deadline lives on disk.

  4. Submit

    acts

    One paced sender with a host allowlist, so a token cannot leave its origin.

  5. Grade

    records

    Deterministic grading that says “unknown” rather than guessing. Every attempt is saved.

What it solved

What worked

  • Score the artifact

    Every pass came from one field the judge reads, such as a sent message or a tool-call argument.

  • Reframe, don’t escalate

    Wins made the target action the natural way to finish the user’s task. Louder wording never beat that.

  • Belief is not obedience

    Agents believed a forged message and still declined. Supplying the missing authorisation changed the result.

  • Read why each run failed

    Removing one objection at a time took the email test from 0 to 9 of 10.

Where it failed

Level 6

About 1,900 attempts and no pass. Three barriers compounded, and the budget ran out first. That is not proof it is impossible.

Email test

A refusal over an unfamiliar domain never went away. I treat it as a floor for that design, not a bug.

What is withheld

  • Payload text
  • Target weaknesses
  • Grader details
  • Platform internals
  • Credentials
  • Target and model names

A working payload is a working attack. Only the shape of the engine and the aggregate results are public.

Limits

  • Two engagements by one person: an anecdote, not a benchmark.
  • Attempt counts mix hand-written, ensemble and replayed payloads, so they do not measure the generator alone.
  • Some captures are duplicated across runs, so counts are approximate.
  • Agents are stochastic. One passing run is not a rate.
